## LinkedIn caption
Neighborhood Spotlight: Elizabeth, Charlotte NC

Elizabeth is a National Register historic district (listed 1989) whose western edge sits about a mile east of Uptown. Charlotte's second streetcar suburb, it grew from five subdivisions laid out between 1891 and 1915 and takes its name from Elizabeth College, which began building here in 1897.

What stands out today:
· Bungalows with Craftsman, Colonial Revival and Tudor Revival touches, plus 1920s and 1930s duplexes and small apartment buildings
· Independence Park, Charlotte's first public park, designed by John Nolen and reopened in 2023 after a nearly $6 million county renovation
· Oaks planted by the early developers, with willow oaks along Clement and Greenway avenues
· CityLYNX Gold Line streetcar stops, including Elizabeth & Hawthorne and Sunnyside Ave.

Market snapshot (Realtor.com, as of October 2026): $701,000 median list price, $870,000 median sold price, $346 per square foot, 40 active listings, 71 median days on market.

My recommendation: in a neighborhood with this much variety, compare a bungalow, a duplex and a larger home on the same visit. Lot size, layout and upkeep are easier to judge side by side.

## Slides
1. ELIZABETH (hook band "CHARLOTTE NC. ELIZABETH."): Oaks and porches, near Uptown. Western edge about one mile east of Uptown; Charlotte's second streetcar suburb laid out 1891–1915; National Register 1989.
2. HISTORY: Named for a college. Elizabeth College (Lutheran college for women) began building in 1897; 20-acre site with a grove at the end of a new Highland Park Company street; college moved to Salem, Virginia, in 1915.
3. STREETCAR SUBURB: Five subdivisions, one neighborhood. Highland Park 1891, Piedmont Park and Oakhurst 1900, Elizabeth Heights 1904, Rosemont 1915; edges followed old farm tracts; streetcar along East Seventh Street and Hawthorne Lane.
4. ARCHITECTURE: Bungalows, Tudor and Colonial. More than half bungalows with Craftsman/Colonial Revival/Tudor Revival touches; Spanish Mission and Italian Renaissance Revival too; 1920s–30s duplexes and small apartment buildings on nearly every street.
5. INDEPENDENCE PARK (real photo): Charlotte's first public park. Established 1905 on the former city waterworks site; designed by John Nolen, one of his earliest commissions, before his 1911 Myers Park plan; Hawthorne Lane splits it in two.
6. PARK TODAY (real photo): Renewed in 2023. 34 acres, reopened June 2023 after a nearly $6M county renovation; 12-ft greenway ties to Hawthorne Lane and Little Sugar Creek Greenway; perennial garden, nature playground, trails, picnic pavilion, athletic fields.
7. TREE CANOPY: A canopy decades in the making. Developers planted mainly oaks; willow oaks along Clement Avenue and Greenway Avenue; East Seventh, Hawthorne and Clement 70+ ft wide.
8. GOLD LINE (real photo): Streetcars, again. Opened Aug 30, 2021, 4 miles, 17 stops; Elizabeth stops include Elizabeth & Hawthorne, Hawthorne & 5th St., 8th St., Sunnyside Ave.; links the Historic West End through Center City to Elizabeth.
9. THE MARKET: Realtor.com as of October 2026: $701,000 median list, $870,000 median sold, $346/sq ft, 40 active listings, 71 median days on market. Only source line on the carousel.
10. MY RECOMMENDATION + contact (stock photo of a tree-lined path in fall, not named): Let's walk it together. Streetcar ride to Hawthorne Lane, then tour bungalows, Tudors and duplexes on the tree-lined side streets.
