## Facts and sources (verified Nov 3 2026)
A. NC Standard Form 2-T, Offer to Purchase and Contract (July 2026 marked changes, https://www.ncrealtors.org/wp-content/uploads/markedup0726-2-T.pdf):
 - para. 4: "There is no loan or appraisal contingency in this Contract"; buyer advised to consult lender so the Due Diligence Period allows time for the loan process (slide 4).
 - para. 4(b)(iv): "Appraisals: An appraisal of the Property" listed as a due diligence item (slide 3).
 - para. 4(g): buyer may terminate for any reason or no reason by written notice during the Due Diligence Period; para. 4 intro: if buyer terminates outside the DD Period, buyer may lose the Earnest Money Deposit (slide 4).
 - para. 6(c): buyer responsible for the cost of the appraisal (item (iii)); para. 6(d): buyer authorizes providing the contract "to any appraiser employed by Buyer or by Buyer's lender(s)" (slide 3).
 - para. 8(c): Seller shall provide reasonable access through the earlier of closing or possession, incl. existing utilities operating at Seller's cost (slide 6).
 - para. 19: all changes, additions or deletions must be in writing and signed by all parties (slide 9).
B. 12 CFR 1026.42 (Regulation Z, valuation independence), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.42 : (c)(1) no covered person (creditor or settlement service provider) may cause the value to be based on anything other than the appraiser's independent judgment, e.g. seeking to influence a minimum value; (c)(3) permitted: asking the appraiser to consider additional, appropriate property information including comparable properties, requesting further detail, asking to correct errors (slides 5, 8, 9). Applies to consumer credit secured by the consumer's principal dwelling.
