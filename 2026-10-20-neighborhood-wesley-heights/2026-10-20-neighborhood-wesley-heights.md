## LinkedIn caption
Neighborhood Spotlight: Wesley Heights, Charlotte NC

Wesley Heights is a National Register historic district about a mile northwest of Trade and Tryon. It began as the J. W. Wadsworth Model Farm, was first laid out in 1911 after the West Trade Street trolley line was extended nearby, and filled in with homes mostly during the 1920s.

What stands out today:
· Bungalow and Craftsman homes, with Tudor Revival and Colonial Revival designs
· Long, narrow lots, brick retaining walls and granite curbs
· The Wesley Heights stop on the CityLYNX Gold Line, open since August 2021
· A county greenway system close by

Market snapshot (Realtor.com, as of October 2026): $499,000 median list price, $552,500 median sold price, $359 per square foot, 23 active listings, 99 median days on market.

My recommendation: if you're weighing a historic home against a newer build, see them side by side in the same neighborhood. The differences in layout, upkeep and lot are easier to judge in person.

## Slides
1. WESLEY HEIGHTS (hook band "CHARLOTTE NC. WESLEY HEIGHTS."): Bungalows, a mile from Uptown. About one mile northwest of Trade and Tryon; National Register 1995; its own Gold Line stop.
2. HISTORY: A livery family's model farm. J. W. Wadsworth's livery stable (largest in Charlotte) and role in the first horse-drawn streetcars; J. W. Wadsworth Model Farm known for Holstein cattle; 1895 Wadsworth Sons Company.
3. STREETCAR SUBURB: Built for the trolley. Farm subdivided ~1910 after the West Trade trolley extension; first laid out 1911; Charlotte Investment Company bought it 1920; lots on sale Dec 1921; houses from 1922.
4. ARCHITECTURE: Craftsman, Tudor and Colonial. Bungalow/Craftsman with Tudor Revival and Colonial Revival; 335 of 372 buildings contributing at the 1995 listing; duplexes and small apartment buildings mixed in.
5. STREETSCAPE: Walls, curbs and canopy. Original brick and cinderblock retaining walls; granite curbstones; mature trees over rolling Piedmont hills.
6. LOTS AND LAYOUT: Long lots, deep yards. Lots mostly 55 ft wide, 150 or 187 ft deep; homes set back with driveways and garages; rear alleys in the older section.
7. GOLD LINE (real photo): Back on the streetcar. Wesley Heights stop opened Aug 30, 2021; Gold Line 4 miles, 17 stops; French Street to Sunnyside Avenue in Elizabeth.
8. OUTDOORS: A greenway close by. Irwin Creek, Stewart Creek and Wesley Heights greenway system; main section 2.7 miles, State Street to Cedar Street; connection to Freedom Drive.
9. THE MARKET: Realtor.com as of October 2026: $499,000 median list, $552,500 median sold, $359/sq ft, 23 active listings, 99 median days on market. Only source line on the carousel.
10. MY RECOMMENDATION + contact: Let's walk it together. Streetcar ride down West Trade, then tour 1920s bungalows and newer homes side by side.
