"Offers are landing close to asking, so the terms can matter as much as the price." (data A; takeaway is my interpretation of the 98% ratio) Source: Realtor.com Economic Research, SouthPark page, Oct 2026
3. NC: WEIGH THE DUE DILIGENCE FEE: on NC's Form 2-T, it's paid to you and due on the Effective Date (B, C); it's yours from that date and credited to the buyer at closing (B, C); it's non-refundable unless the contract says otherwise or you materially breach (B)
4. CHECK THE EARNEST MONEY: NC: it goes to the escrow agent within five days of the Effective Date (B, initial deposit); NC: if the buyer ends the deal during due diligence, it's refunded to them (B ¶8(g), D); SC: a broker holds it in a separate trust account until closing or termination, S.C. Code 40-57-136 (F)
5. ASK HOW THEY'LL PAY: Cash offer? NC's Form 2-T advises sellers to get proof of funds before signing (B ¶5(a)); a preapproval letter helps, but it isn't a guaranteed loan offer (G); NC's Form 2-T has no loan or appraisal contingency, and the buyer's due diligence period covers that (B ¶4(a), D)
6. READ THE CONTINGENCIES: NC: the standard contract isn't contingent on the buyer selling their home; that takes a custom addendum (B ¶5(b), H2); SC: every term and condition belongs in the written offer, S.C. Code 40-57-135 (F); know exactly which conditions you're agreeing to (my tip)
7. MATCH THE DATES TO YOUR MOVE: NC: settlement happens no later than the contract date, at a time and place the buyer picks (B ¶1(g), 7/2026 wording); possession transfers at closing unless an addendum says otherwise (B ¶13); need a few days after closing? ask about a Seller Possession After Closing Agreement, Form 2A8-T (B ¶13, I)
8. ADD UP THE CONCESSIONS: NC: seller concessions sit on page 1 of Form 2-T, as dollars or a percent (B ¶1(e), H2); buyer agent compensation (Form 220) is a concession too, in addition to those (H, B ¶1(e)); ask for a net sheet on each offer so you compare what you'd keep (my tip; NC REALTORS Form 110 Seller Estimated Net Sheet exists, I)
9. MULTIPLE OFFERS? YOU DECIDE: there's no "first in the door" rule; you choose which offer to work with (E); you can ask some or all buyers for their highest and best (E); any change you make is a counteroffer, and it rejects the original offer (D)
10. MY RECOMMENDATION: COMPARE OFFERS SIDE BY SIDE. "I recommend one page per offer with your estimated net, so you decide on the full picture.
