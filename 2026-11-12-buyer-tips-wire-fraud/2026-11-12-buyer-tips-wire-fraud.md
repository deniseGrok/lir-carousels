## Slides (copy lives in build/build_slides.py)
1 Cover: "PROTECT YOUR" / script "closing funds" / "Practical advice for BUYERS". Image gen-01.
2 How the scam works: scammers pose as your agent, closing attorney or another trusted contact and email fake wiring instructions, often as a last-minute change. Bullets: Look-alike email addresses / Copied names and formats / "Updated" wire details / Timed close to closing. Source A.
3 Set the plan before closing: talk through how you'll send closing funds, in person or by phone, early; write down two trusted contacts and their numbers; a code phrase can help. Bullets: Talk it through early / Name two trusted contacts / Save their numbers now / Agree on a code phrase. Source A.
4 Call a number you trust: before you wire, call the closing attorney's office at a publicly verified number and confirm the instructions with them directly. Bullets: Use a verified number / Never the one in the email / Confirm name and account / Speak with the office. Sources A, B.
5 Treat any change as a red flag: closing attorneys rarely change wiring instructions mid-closing; an NCREC bulletin says to treat any change request as fraud until verified by phone. Bullets: Don't reply to the email / Don't click the links / Call to verify first / Faxes can be faked too. Source C (#4, #5), A (avoid links/numbers in email).
6 Keep money talk off email: email is never a secure way to send financial information; scammers can copy email addresses, phone numbers and formats. Bullets: Don't email account numbers / Skip links and attachments / Caller ID can be faked / When unsure, call back. Sources A, C (#5 caller ID spoofing).
7 Confirm it arrived: in NC, wire only to the law firm's trust account, in a name that matches the firm; then call the office at a known number to confirm receipt. Bullets: Trust account only / Name matches the firm / Call to confirm receipt / Use a known number. Sources C (#6, #8), F.
8 If it happens, act fast: call your bank right away and ask for a wire recall, then file a complaint at ic3.gov; reporting fast can improve the odds of recovery. Bullets: Call your bank now / Ask for a wire recall / File at ic3.gov / Then call your attorney. Sources A, D, E. "Then call your attorney" is a recommendation in Denise's voice.
9 Why it matters, FBI IC3 2025 (navy stats, no photo): Real estate fraud complaints 12,368 (+32% vs 2024's 9,359); real estate fraud losses $275.1M ($275,110,419; +58% vs 2024's $173,586,820); BEC losses all sectors $3.05B ($3,046,598,558; 24,768 complaints); frozen via the FBI's domestic Financial Fraud Kill Chain $507M ($507,042,623; 3,574 incidents). Body: the FBI says time is of the essence: contact your bank and request a recall as soon as you find a fraudulent transfer. Source line on slide. Source D.

## Facts and sources (all verified live Thu Nov 12 2026, ~4:25-4:50 AM ET)
A. CFPB blog, "Mortgage Closing Scams: How to protect yourself and your closing funds" (Jun 3 2019): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/mortgage-closing-scams-how-protect-yourself-and-your-closing-funds/ (spoofed emails posing as agent/settlement agent with false wiring instructions, last-minute changes; identify two trusted individuals; discuss in person or by phone; write down names and numbers; code phrase; confirm by a previously verified phone number; avoid numbers/links in email; email is never secure for financial info; if it happens contact your bank immediately and ask for a wire recall; file at ic3.gov).
B. NCREC "Real Estate Closings" brochure: https://www.ncrec.gov/Brochures/ClosingsBrochure.pdf (call the closing attorney's office at a publicly verified number and speak directly with the attorney or staff; don't rely on emails, texts or calls claiming to be them).
C. NCREC Bulletin 2018-V48-3, "When Hackers Strike" (Feb 16 2018): https://bulletins.ncrec.gov/when-hackers-strike/ (#4 assume any change request is fraudulent, verify by phone; closing attorneys won't change instructions mid-closing except in extreme circumstances; #5 faxes are no safer, caller ID can be spoofed; #6 wire only to the law firm's trust account, name matching the firm; #8 call the office afterward to confirm receipt).
D. FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report (released Apr 2026): https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf (slide 9 figures; "If you discover a fraudulent transfer, time is of the essence. Immediately, contact your financial institution and request a recall"; file at ic3.gov regardless of amount; Real Estate category includes rental and timeshare fraud).
E. IC3 Domestic Financial Fraud Kill Chain brochure: https://www.ic3.gov/Outreach/Brochures/IC3-FFKC_Domestic.pdf.
F. NAR consumer guide on wire fraud: https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-how-to-protect-against-real-estate-wire-fraud (secondary; call a trusted source before and after wiring).
SC: no SC-specific regulator wire guidance used; tips are framed generally (CFPB/FBI apply in both states); the trust-account line on slide 7 is labeled "In NC". Not legal advice.
