### Slide 3: History
- SC Encyclopedia (https://www.scencyclopedia.org/sce/entries/fort-mill/): The Catawbas and Cherokees asked for forts. "no work was ever done on the Fort Mill fort," but the planned fort gave the town half its name. The other half came from "a grist mill on Steele Creek established during the 1770s or 1780s by Isaac Garrison and Theodoric Webb." The name changed to Fort Mill in 1830. The General Assembly chartered the town on Feb. 12, 1873. Fort Mill Manufacturing Company was chartered Jan. 23, 1887. The plants were "combined to form the Springs Cotton Mills in 1931." The mill closed in 1983.
- SC Legislature Bill 4129 (sesquicentennial resolution) (https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess125_2023-2024/bills/4129.htm): The name comes from "a fort constructed by the British in colonial times" and "Webb's Mill, a grist mill on nearby Steele Creek." Established 1873.
- NPS MPS form (above): The name "honored the old grist mill... and a small garrison fort begun by the British in the 1750s." The railroad was completed through the area in 1852.
- NCSU Textiles History (https://history.textiles.ncsu.edu/textile-companies/springs-industries-inc/): Fort Mill Mfg. Co. was incorporated in 1887 (founder Samuel Elliott White). Springs consolidated into Springs Cotton Mills in 1933. **This conflicts with the Encyclopedia's 1931, so the copy leaves out the merger year.**
- The sources disagree on whether the fort was ever built (Encyclopedia says no work was done; the NPS form says it was "begun"). The copy says only "a colonial-era fort."

### Slide 4: Historic downtown
- NPGallery NRIS 92000646 (https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/92000646): Fort Mill Downtown Historic District. It runs along Main St. from Confederate Park east to 233 Main. Listed June 11, 1992. Style category: "Late 19th and 20th Century Revivals." Areas of significance: commerce and architecture.
- SCDAH (https://www.nationalregister.sc.gov/york/S10817746036/index.htm): 22 contributing and 4 noncontributing resources, "predominantly of masonry one and two-story commercial buildings facing Main Street and constructed between 1860 and 1940." Listed June 11, 1992.
- NRHP nomination form (https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/95efba98-9b0a-4b27-ab67-d39c181eb9ed/): Most buildings date from rebuilding after fires (1894-1940).
- NPS MPS form: "good examples are at 210 Main Street, 223 Main Street, and 225 Main Street, all of which feature brick panels and corbelling and date from around 1900."
- Note: this is a commercial district, not homes. I suggest the pill "THE BUILDINGS" or "HISTORIC DOWNTOWN" instead of "THE HOMES."

### Slide 5: Greenway
- ASC Greenway Visit page (https://www.ascgreenway.org/visit/): "Opened to the community in 1995... over 2,100 acres." It also says "more than 40 miles of trails." Main entrances are Lake Haigler, Adventure Road and the Dairy Barn.
- ASC Greenway home page (https://www.ascgreenway.org/): "2,100 acres," "40 miles of trails," and "more than twice the size of Central Park."
- Entrances page (https://www.ascgreenway.org/visit/entrances/): Hours are 7 a.m. to sunset. "Daily Usage Fees: Adult $17 per person | Youth (ages 3-12) $8 per person. Free to Members." Lake Haigler points of interest include the kayak launch, the Swinging Bridge and Historic Nation Ford Road. Dairy Barn points of interest include the Founders Dairy Barn, Faires-Coltharp Cabin, Graham Cabin and the Comporium Amphitheater.
- FAQ (https://www.ascgreenway.org/visit/visitor-faqs/): Confirms the daily admission fee of $17 per adult and $8 for ages 3-12. **It says "36-miles of trails," which conflicts with 40.** The copy uses "40 miles," backed by the home page, the Visit page and the 2026 Trail Passport.
- 2026 Trail Passport (https://www.ascgreenway.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ASCG_TrailsPassport2026_web.pdf): "over 40 miles of hiking, biking and equestrian trails." The Haigler Loop is 1.75 mi around Lake Haigler, "the largest scenic lake on the Greenway." It names the Steele Creek Swinging Bridge. Blue Star Trail (7.8 mi) follows the historic Nation Ford Road.

### Slide 8: My tip (4% legal-residence ratio)
- S.C. Code §12-43-220 (https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c043.php):
  - (c)(1): A legal residence plus up to 5 contiguous acres is "taxed on an assessment equal to four percent of the fair market value."
  - (e): "All other real property... six percent."
  - (c)(2)(ii): The 4% ratio does not apply unless the owner or agent applies "before the first penalty date for the payment of taxes for the tax year for which the owner first claims eligibility."
  - (c)(2)(iv): The assessor may require proof, including the owner's most recent SC income tax return and SC vehicle registrations at that address.
  - (c)(2)(vi): A new owner must file a new application.
  - (c)(2)(viii): Failing to file in time abandons the classification for the current tax year.
- York County Assessor (https://www.yorkcountysc.gov/150/Assessor): Lists the "Legal Residence Application," phone 803-684-8526.
- York County Legal Residence Application (https://www.yorkcountysc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9731/Legal-Residence-Application): Incomplete applications without supporting documents are returned. "Please allow ten days for processing."
- "Jan. 15" as the penalty date appeared only on a non-official site (legalclarity.org), so the specific date is CUT.
