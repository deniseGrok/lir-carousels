## Facts and sources (verified Nov 2 2026)
A. CFPB, "What is homeowner's insurance? Why is homeowner's insurance required?" (last reviewed Aug 8 2024), https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-homeowners-insurance-why-is-homeowners-insurance-required-en-162/ : lenders generally require proof of homeowner's insurance; also called hazard insurance; many pay through escrow; cost listed on page 1 of the Loan Estimate (Projected Payments); you can shop separately and choose the provider; not the same as mortgage insurance; standard policies don't cover earthquake or flood (slides 3, 4, 8).
B. NC Standard Form 2-T (July 2026 marked changes, https://www.ncrealtors.org/wp-content/uploads/markedup0726-2-T.pdf): due diligence item (iii) "Insurance: Investigation of the availability and cost of insurance for the Property" (slide 4); para. 9(c) seller authorizes the owners' association / management company / insurance company to release the master insurance policy showing coverage and deductible amount (slide 9).
C. C.L.U.E.: Wisconsin OCI PI-207 FAQ (https://oci.wi.gov/Documents/Consumers/PI-207.pdf), Utah Insurance Dept FAQ (https://insurance.utah.gov/consumers/clue/), Washington OIC (https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/auto-insurance/credit-and-insurance/clue-comprehensive-loss-underwriting-exchange): up to seven years of personal property claims; only owner, insurer or lender can access a property's report, a buyer can ask the current owner to order it; one free copy per 12 months (FACT Act) from LexisNexis; insurers may request it when you apply or ask for a quote (slide 5).
D. NC DOI, Actual Cash Value vs. Replacement Cost Value (https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers/homeowners-insurance/actual-cash-value-vs-replacement-cost-value) and Consumer's Guide to Homeowner's Insurance (https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers-guide-homeowners-insurance/open): RCV = cost at today's prices, ACV subtracts depreciation for age/use; most policies cover contents on ACV, many insurers offer a replacement cost option (HO 04 90 endorsement); higher deductibles lower premium (slides 6, 7).
E. NC DOI FAQs About Homeowners Insurance (https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers/homeowners-insurance/faqs-about-homeowners-insurance): deductibles can be fixed or a percentage of dwelling coverage; separate windstorm, hail or named-storm deductibles possible; review the declarations page (slide 7).
F. NC DOI Homeowners Insurance (https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers/homeowners-insurance): some insurers' underwriting excludes windstorm or hail and often flood; ask about wind/hail protection (slide 8).
SC: slide 4 says only "In SC, read your contract too" (a recommendation, no SC rule claimed). Slides 4 ("Get quotes on the home"), 5 ("Ask early, not at closing"), 7 ("Pick one you can cover"), 9 ("Cover the gaps with your policy", "Ask your insurance agent") and 10 are planning recommendations in Denise's voice. Not insurance, legal or lending advice; slide 9 points buyers to their insurance agent.
