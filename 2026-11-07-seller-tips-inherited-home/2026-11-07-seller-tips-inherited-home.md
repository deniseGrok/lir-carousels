3 Confirm who can sign. Body: NC: title passes to heirs or devisees at death (G.S. 28A-15-2); an executor sells under the will or a clerk proceeding (G.S. 28A-15-1). SC: will authority or court process (S.C. Code 62-3-711). Bullets: Executor or the heirs? / Will's power of sale? / NC: heirs' spouses on deed / Ask the estate attorney. Sources A, B, C, F.
4 Gather the paperwork. Body: You may need proof of your right to the home, such as the will, the death certificate, or a letter from the executor (CFPB). Bullets: Death certificate / Will + executor letters / Recorded deed / Mortgage + tax bills. Source D (deed, mortgage and tax bills are the property records an attorney and servicer work from; listed as practical items, no rule claimed).
5 Tell the servicer. Body: Send a written notice. Under the CFPB servicing rule, the servicer must describe the documents it needs to confirm you as a successor in interest. Bullets: Write to the servicer / Name, address, loan no. / Ask what proof it needs / Ask how to keep paying. Sources D, E.
6 Protect the empty house. Body: Tell the insurer about the death and any vacancy. One standard form limits vandalism and glass cover after 60 days vacant in a row (AAIS HO 0003 01 06). Bullets: Tell the insurer early / Ask about vacancy limits / Heat on, or water off / Check on it often. Source G ("one standard form" wording because policies vary; the Denise-facing advice is to ask her insurer).
7 Disclosures still matter. Body: NC and SC exempt a fiduciary's sale during estate administration (G.S. 47E-2(a)(3); S.C. Code 27-50-30(3)). Heirs selling are not exempt, and brokers still disclose material facts. Bullets: Fiduciary sale: exempt / Heirs selling: disclose / Known facts still count / Confirm with your attorney. Sources H, I, F, J.
8 Know your tax basis. Body: Basis is generally fair market value at the date of death (IRS Pub. 551, Dec 2025). Inherited property counts as held over 1 year (IRS Pub. 544, 2025). Bullets: Date-of-death value / Keep the appraisal / Long-term holding rule / Talk with a CPA. Sources L1, L2.
9 Agree first, then list. Body: I recommend every owner agree in writing on price, repairs, and who covers carrying costs, then price from recent solds. Bullets: Every owner on board / Agree on price + repairs / Split carrying costs / Price from recent solds. Denise's recommendation (advice, no rule or number claimed); NC REALTORS Q&A (F) shows heirs and the personal representative all signing the listing.

## Facts and sources (verified live Sat Nov 7, 2026 ~4:15–4:45 AM ET)
A. N.C. Gen. Stat. 28A-15-2(b): title to a decedent's real property vests in the heirs at death; property devised under a valid probated will vests in the devisees, relating back to death. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_28A/GS_28A-15-2.html
B. N.C. Gen. Stat. 28A-15-1(c) (and 28A-17-1): to sell real estate the personal representative institutes a special proceeding before the clerk of superior court, except no proceeding is required for a sale under authority given by will (a general power of sale in the will suffices). https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_28A/GS_28A-15-1.html
C. S.C. Code 62-3-711(b),(c): except where the will authorizes it, a personal representative may not sell estate real property except under the procedures in 62-3-911 or 62-3-1301 et seq.; 62-3-101: real property devolves to devisees or heirs at death. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c003.php
D. CFPB, "How do I get mortgage information about a home I inherited?": inform the servicer; you may need proof of your right to the home, e.g., the executed will and death certificate or a letter from the executor; the servicer should tell you what proof it needs; ask how to continue making payments; consider an attorney or HUD-approved housing counselor. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-get-mortgage-information-about-a-home-i-inherited-en-217/
E. CFPB Executive Summary of the 2016 Mortgage Servicing Rule (Aug 4, 2016): a servicer must respond to a written request from a potential successor in interest that names the borrower and identifies the loan, with a written description of the documents it reasonably requires to confirm identity and ownership interest, plus contact info (12 CFR 1024.36(i), 1024.38(b)(1)(vi)); confirmed successors generally get the same servicing protections. https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/08042016_cfpb_Mortgage_Servicing_Executive_Summary.pdf
F. NC REALTORS Legal Q&A "Estate listings and Disclosure Statements" (released 01/21/2015): the 47E-2(a)(3) exemption covers only the personal representative (executor/administrator); heirs are not exempt and must sign the disclosure statement; heirs' spouses will most certainly have to sign any deed to release potential marital rights. Also "Use of Disclosure Statement in Estate Sale": transfers by beneficiaries are not exempt; check with the estate attorney. https://www.ncrealtors.org/question/estate-listings-and-disclosure-statements/ · https://www.ncrealtors.org/question/use-of-disclosure-statement-in-estate-sale/
G. AAIS Homeowners Special Form HO 0003 01 06: vandalism/malicious mischief (and the glass-breakage coverage) excluded if the residence was vacant for more than 60 days in a row just before the loss; freezing exclusion does not apply if reasonable care is taken to maintain heat or shut off the water and drain systems. https://glmutual.com/docs/AAIS/HO%200003.pdf (one bureau form; individual policies vary — slide says "one standard form").
H. N.C. Gen. Stat. 47E-2(a)(3): exempt — transfers by a fiduciary in the course of the administration of a decedent's estate, guardianship, conservatorship, or trust. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_47E/GS_47E-2.html
I. S.C. Code 27-50-30(3): the disclosure article does not apply to transfers by a fiduciary in the course of the administration of a decedent's estate. https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c050.php
J. NCREC Bulletin "Rolling out the Revised RPOADS!": a broker's duty to disclose is separate from the owner's; brokers must disclose material facts they know or reasonably should know. https://bulletins.ncrec.gov/rolling-out-the-revised-rpoads/
K. Realtor.com Economic Research metro core metrics CSV (downloaded Nov 7, 2026 ~4:30 AM ET), row 202610 / CBSA 16740 Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia, NC-SC: median_listing_price 419,900 (yy −0.0413); median_days_on_market 65 (yy +0.1111); pending_listing_count 4,129 (yy −0.1299); price_reduced_share 0.2714 (yy +0.0004). https://econdata.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Reports/Core/RDC_Inventory_Core_Metrics_Metro.csv
L1. IRS Publication 551 (12/2025), Basis of Assets, "Inherited Property": basis generally the FMV at the date of death (or alternate valuation date if elected); Schedule A (Form 8971) consistent-basis rule when an estate tax return is required. https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
L2. IRS Publication 544 (2025), Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets: "If you inherit property, you are considered to have held the property longer than 1 year, regardless of how long you actually held it." https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544
