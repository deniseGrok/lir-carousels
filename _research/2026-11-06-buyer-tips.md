## 2. Exact 10-slide outline (cover, 7 tip beats, stats, CTA)

Design: Signature Authority v2 (Buyer/Seller Tips only). No interest rates → no APR line.

### Slide 1 — COVER
**Headline idea:** READ THE HOA DOCS  
**Sub:** Practical advice for BUYERS  
**Beat:** Carolinas communities often come with mandatory associations. The disclosure form is a start — the governing documents are where the real rules and costs live.  
**Claim type:** framing / no statute claim on cover.

### Slide 2 — Ask for the full packet (not just the form)
**Tip:** Before or during due diligence, get the declaration/restrictive covenants, bylaws, rules & regulations, articles, current budget + financial statement, architectural guidelines, parking rules, and the association’s master insurance info.  
**Verified claim:** NC Form **2A12-T** (Owners’ Association Disclosure Addendum, Revised 7/2025 © 7/2026) §6 authorizes release to Buyer of: Seller’s statement of account; master insurance policy (coverage + deductible); Declaration and Restrictive Covenants; Rules and Regulations; Articles of Incorporation; Bylaws; current financial statement and budget; parking restrictions; architectural guidelines. Seller representations are to best of Seller’s knowledge; Buyer should confirm during Due Diligence Period.  
**Also:** NCREC *Questions and Answers on: Due Diligence for Residential Buyers* (REC 4.26, 7/31/20) — “Documents to Review: Restrictive Covenants and Homeowners’ Association Bylaws” — obtain and review available covenants/HOA docs; inquire about HOA financial situation and whether anticipated expenses could lead to an assessment.  
**Sources:** Form 2A12-T §6 (7/2026); NCREC Due Diligence brochure REC 4.26 (7/31/20).

### Slide 3 — Know the dues — and what they actually cover
**Tip:** Match the regular assessment amount to what the association pays for (insurance, landscaping, roads, amenities, management, etc.). Factor dues into affordability before you offer.  
**Verified claim:** Form 2A12-T §§1–2 require Seller to name each association, state regular assessments (“dues”) amount and period, and check which services/amenities are paid from dues (master insurance, common-area taxes, exterior maintenance, private road maintenance, recreational amenities, etc.). NCREC Subdivisions brochure: if membership is mandatory, you must pay all lawful assessments/dues/charges; there is no fixed statutory cap on what an owner may have to pay so long as charges are lawfully imposed under the covenants — prospective purchasers should consider current/pending dues and the association’s financial health.  
**SC:** SC Department of Consumer Affairs *HOA: Before You Buy* — look for required fees; factor association dues into affordability; ask what you get for your money.  
**Sources:** Form 2A12-T §§1–2 (7/2026); NCREC *Residential Subdivisions and Planned Communities* (REC 3.49, 11/1/19); SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*.

### Slide 4 — Reserves and special assessments
**Tip:** Thin reserves can mean a special assessment. Ask what’s in reserve, when dues last rose, and whether any special assessment is approved or likely.  
**Verified claim:** NCREC Condos & Townhouses brochure (REC 3.31, 3/1/16): before signing, examine governing documents for maintenance fees/assessments and whether they may increase; find out financial stability of the association, whether dues/reserves are sufficient for larger future expenditures, and whether there are any pending or proposed assessments. Form 2A12-T §3: Seller represents there are no other dues, fees, or Special Assessments payable by owners as of the date, except those listed. REC 4.22 REV 7/26 Section H1(c): seller must disclose duly approved changes to dues, fees, or special assessments to which the lot is subject (nature and amount). SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*: find out how much is in reserve; if HOA lacks funds for capital improvements they may impose a special assessment; ask how often dues change and when last increase/special assessment occurred.  
**Sources:** NCREC Condos & Townhouses REC 3.31 (3/1/16); Form 2A12-T §3 (7/2026); NCREC REC 4.22 REV 7/26 §H1(c); SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*.  
**Note for copy:** Do **not** invent a required reserve-study statute for NC or SC — neither state has a general statutory reserve-study mandate for planned communities; stick to “ask for budget/financials/reserves.”

### Slide 5 — Rental, pet, parking, and use rules
**Tip:** Read the rules for rentals, pets, parking, exterior changes, home businesses, RVs/boats, and flags/signs. Buying the home means agreeing to live under those covenants.  
**Verified claim:** NCREC Due Diligence brochure: review covenants/HOA docs to verify intended use is permitted — examples include operating a business from the home, renting the home, paint colors, parking, or having animals. NCREC Subdivisions brochure: display of signs/flags/banners, certain uses, storage of personal property (e.g., boats, RVs), keeping of animals, and other practices can lawfully be controlled by properly created/enforced restrictive covenants; purchaser who does not abide can be fined; under Planned Community Act, association may impose fine up to $100 per day after notice and hearing. NCREC Condos brochure: association rules can substantially affect use and can restrict ability to rent the unit; carefully read rules before purchase. SC DCA: check restrictions including landscaping, building standards, guests, garbage, parking, and pets.  
**Sources:** NCREC Due Diligence REC 4.26 (7/31/20); NCREC Subdivisions REC 3.49 (11/1/19); NCREC Condos & Townhouses REC 3.31 (3/1/16); SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*.  
**Fair Housing:** describe *document topics* (rental/pet/parking rules exist) — never imply who a home “suits,” never target familial status, and never stereotype.

### Slide 6 — Master insurance, deductibles, and transfer fees
**Tip:** Ask what the association’s master policy covers, what the deductible is, and what you still need on an HO-6 / homeowners policy. Also get every transfer/resale/move-in fee in writing.  
**Verified claim:** Form 2A12-T §6 lists master insurance policy showing coverage provided and deductible amount among documents Seller authorizes for release. Form 2A12-T §5 requires disclosure of fees charged by the association or management company in connection with transfer (document prep, move in/out, insurance docs, statement of unpaid assessments, transfer fees). REC 4.22 §H2 asks whether any fee is charged in connection with conveyance/transfer and the amount. SC DCA: ask to see the association’s insurance declaration page.  
**Sources:** Form 2A12-T §§5–6 (7/2026); REC 4.22 REV 7/26 §H2; SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*.

### Slide 7 — Minutes, lawsuits, and “am I buying someone else’s violation?”
**Tip:** Request recent board minutes/newsletters, ask whether the property is currently in violation, and read any disclosed judgments or pending lawsuits involving the property or the association.  
**Verified claim:** Form 2A12-T §4: Seller represents there are no unsatisfied judgments against or pending lawsuits involving the Property, the Development, and/or the owners’ association, except as listed. REC 4.22 §§H3–H4: disclose unsatisfied judgment, pending lawsuit, or existing/alleged governing-document violation involving the property; and unsatisfied judgments/pending lawsuits against the association. SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*: request association meeting minutes from the past 12 months and newsletters; ask for something in writing from the HOA stating the home is currently not in violation; attend a meeting if possible; search for complaints/lawsuits; consult an attorney if needed.  
**Sources:** Form 2A12-T §4 (7/2026); REC 4.22 REV 7/26 §§H3–H4; SC DCA *HOA: Before You Buy*.

### Slide 8 — Condo extras (NC) + do this inside Due Diligence
**Tip:** New NC condos can include a public offering statement and a 7-day cancel right; many resales get a fee statement. Either way, finish document review before the NC Due Diligence Period ends.  
**Verified claims:**
- **New condo POS + cancel:** G.S. 47C-4-102 / 47C-4-108 — person required to deliver a public offering statement must provide it before contract execution; no conveyance until seven calendar days after contract execution; purchaser has absolute right to cancel during that seven-calendar-day period without penalty; payments refunded. (Also summarized in NCREC Condos brochure.)
- **Resale fee statement:** G.S. 47C-4-109 — except where POS delivery is required (or exempt), unit owner shall furnish prospective purchaser **before conveyance** a statement of the monthly common expense assessment and any other fees payable by unit owners. (NCREC brochure calls this a “resale certificate.”)
- **Timing:** NCREC Due Diligence Q&A bulletin — buyer investigates anything bearing on whether to move forward or terminate during the Form 2-T Due Diligence Period; termination during DD is buyer’s unilateral written notice; buyer typically recovers earnest money but not the Due Diligence Fee unless otherwise negotiated. Form 2A12-T instructs Buyer to confirm association info during Due Diligence Period.  
**Sources:** G.S. 47C-4-102, 47C-4-108, 47C-4-109; NCREC Condos & Townhouses REC 3.31 (3/1/16); NCREC Bulletin “Due Diligence” Q&A; Form 2A12-T intro + §6 (7/2026).  
**SC note for caption/agent language (not a invented process):** SC buyers should use their contract’s diligence/inspection window and the SC DCA checklist; governing docs are typically the recorded master deed/declaration and bylaws under the Horizontal Property Act / SC HOA frameworks — confirm with closing attorney; do not invent SC cancel-day counts.
