## Facts and sources (verified Oct 31 2026)
A. Deed transfers ownership ("title"); title insurance covers claims from before purchase such as a previous owner's unpaid taxes or unpaid contractors; owner's policy optional, protects your investment; same provider for both policies usually lowers total cost. CFPB "What is owner's title insurance?" https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-owners-title-insurance-en-164/ (slides 3, 4, 5)
B. Lender's title insurance usually required; protects the lender's loan only, not your equity. CFPB "What is lender's title insurance?" https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-lenders-title-insurance-en-163/ (slide 4)
C. Title services (title insurance, title search) can usually be shopped; services listed in section C, page 2 of the Loan Estimate; lender must give a list of local providers; some states, particularly in the Northeast and South, require a closing attorney. CFPB "Shop for title insurance and other closing services" https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/shop-for-title-insurance-and-other-closing-services/ (slides 5, 9)
D. Title search = public records search. NCREC Prelicensing Course Syllabus (Title Examination: public records search) http://www.ncrec.gov/Forms/Education/Prelicense_Course_Syllabus.pdf (slide 3)
E. NC Standard Form 2-T (rev. 7/2026, NC REALTORS / NC Bar) para. 4(b): survey to determine easements, setbacks, property boundaries; review of restrictive covenants, bylaws, rules and other owners' association documents; recommendation to check whether the association charges fees for information. (slides 6, 8)
F. NC Residential Property and Owners' Association Disclosure Statement, REC 4.22 (REV 7/26): E4 easements, shared driveways, party walls, encroachments; E5/E6 private roads and maintenance agreements; G1 lawsuits, liens, judgments that could affect title; G3 covenants, conditions, restrictions. https://www.ncrec.gov/Forms/Consumer/rec422.pdf (slide 7, 8)
G. SC disclosure statement must cover zoning laws, restrictive covenants, building codes, other land-use restrictions and encroachments. S.C. Code Ann. 27-50-40(A)(5) https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c050.php (slide 7)
Slides 1, 6 ("order it early"), 9 and 10 are planning recommendations in Denise's voice, not legal advice; slide 10 points to a real estate attorney for legal questions about title. No premiums, costs or claim statistics used.
