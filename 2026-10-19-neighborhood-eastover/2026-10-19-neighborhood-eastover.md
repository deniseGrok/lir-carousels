## Slides
1. CURVING DRIVES, GRAND HOMES. (hook band: CHARLOTTE NC. EASTOVER.) A residential neighborhood southeast of Uptown, ZIP 28207; bordered by Providence Road, Briar Creek and Randolph Road; home to Mint Museum Randolph (C, B)
2. PLANNED IN 1927. (tag HISTORY) Begun by the E. C. Griffith Company in 1927; laid out on more than 300 acres of former dairy farmland; planned to compete with the already established Myers Park (A)
3. BUILT FOR THE AUTOMOBILE. (tag STREET PLAN) Curving drives by planner Earle Sumner Draper; Charlotte's first automobile suburb, as earlier suburbs had streetcar service; Cherokee Road is its backbone, a gentle curve off Providence Road (A, D)
4. BRICK, SLATE AND COLUMNS. (tag GEORGIAN REVIVAL) Alexander-Howell House (ca. 1929), 250 Cherokee Road, by architect Franklin Gordon; red brick, a slate roof and a curved Doric portico; a historic landmark since 2024, and Georgian-style homes still line Cherokee Road (E, F)
5. TUDOR REVIVAL IN STONE. (tag HAMILTON JONES HOUSE) 201 Cherokee Road, construction began in 1929; one of Charlotte's finest examples of the Tudor Revival style; architect Martin E. Boyer, known for Colonial, Tudor and Georgian designs (A)
6. NORTH CAROLINA'S FIRST ART MUSEUM. (tag MINT MUSEUM RANDOLPH) First branch of the U.S. Mint, which opened uptown in 1836; rebuilt here in 1934-36 on land given by E. C. Griffith; free admission Wednesday nights, 5 to 9 PM (B, G)
7. A CREEKSIDE WALK. (tag OUTDOORS) Briar Creek Greenway: about one paved mile from Randolph Road to Meadowbrook Road; it starts at Mint Museum Randolph and follows Briar Creek; a pedestrian bridge carries the trail across the creek (H, I)
8. A 1929 FIREHOUSE, STILL ON DUTY. (tag FIRE STATION NO. 6) 249 S. Laurel Avenue, designed by architect C. C. Hook; one of three pre-World War II stations still working as firehouses in Charlotte; brick and stone, arched engine bays and a green tile roof (J, K)
9. WHAT HOMES COST HERE (Realtor.com, as of October 2026): median list $2,422,500; median sold $1,995,000; $603/sq ft; 20 active listings; median 55 days on market (L)

## LinkedIn
Eastover was begun in 1927 by the E. C. Griffith Company on more than 300 acres of former dairy farmland, planned to compete with the already established Myers Park. Planner Earle Sumner Draper laid out its curving drives, and it became Charlotte's first automobile suburb at a time when earlier suburbs relied on streetcars. Along Cherokee Road you'll find local landmarks like the Georgian Revival Alexander-Howell House and the Tudor Revival Hamilton Jones House, and at the neighborhood's edge, Mint Museum Randolph, which opened in 1936 as North Carolina's first art museum. Realtor.com shows a $2,422,500 median list price and a $1,995,000 median sold price as of October 2026, with 20 active listings and a median of 55 days on market.
