## Sources (fetched/verified Oct 31, 2026, ~4:45–5:00 AM ET)
- Realtor.com Economic Research RDC_Inventory_Core_Metrics_Zip.csv (econdata.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/Reports/Core/, Last-Modified Sep 30 2026 14:41 GMT; latest month 202609), ZIP 28204, month 202609: median listing price $520,000; median list price/sq ft $348; active listings 43; median days on market 65; quality_flag 1 (Realtor.com flags the row; small-ZIP volatility). ZIP 28204 also covers Elizabeth and parts of Midtown, so the slide labels the numbers by ZIP, not as Cherry-only.
- hmdb.org marker 237679 "Cherry Neighborhood History" (Mecklenburg County Park and Recreation + Cherry Neighborhood Association; Baldwin Ave. at Main St.): platted 1891 by John Springs Myers and Mary Morgan Rawlinson Myers from their 1,000-acre cotton farm; name said to be inspired by the cherry trees on the hillsides of the hollow; Morgan Park renamed Cherry Neighborhood Park July 18, 2006. Linked WFAE (Oct 2021): "less than a mile south of uptown".
- Charlotte-Mecklenburg Historic Landmarks Commission, "The Cherry Neighborhood" (Thomas W. Hanchett, Nov 1984): 1891 plat; Luther, Main and Cherry streets; name from cherry trees; early one-story gable-roofed cottages and hip-roofed duplexes, each with a front porch; Bungalow-style dwellings after WWI; Kings Drive along Cherry's western boundary; Charlottetown Mall completed 1958 at Kings-Independence, one of the first enclosed shopping centers in the South.
- City of Charlotte, Midtown Morehead Cherry Area Plan, "Historical Background" (charmeck.org): platted at least 20 years before Myers Park (Myers Park begun 1911); first homes on Cherry, Main and Luther streets; Kings Drive built late 1940s along Cherry's western border following Little Sugar Creek; Charlottetowne Mall built ~1958 partly by channelizing and covering the creek, one of the first enclosed malls in the South; Metropolitan opened in phases beginning 2006 with the creek restoration; greenway 7th St. to Morehead St.
- Keeping Watch (keepingwatch.org, "Little Sugar: The creek the city loved to hate"): Charlottetown Mall grand opening Oct 28, 1959; its concrete parking lot was atop Little Sugar Creek, "in the dark for more than 40 years"; mall came down late 2005; restoration added meanders, riffles, rain gardens and natural plantings; greenway segment opened 2010.
- UNC Charlotte Urban Institute, Feb 18, 2021 ("Updated maps let you explore Charlotte's changing housing", Quality of Life Explorer): average heated living space of single-family units in Cherry rose from 1,285 to 2,054 sq ft, 2011–2019.
- charlottetrailofhistory.org (map): Trail of History statues along the Little Sugar Creek Greenway / Kings Drive between Seventh Street and Morehead Street; Captain Jack is statue 9. UNC DocSouth Commemorative Landscapes #941: Spirit of Mecklenburg, Capt. James Jack, bronze equestrian by Chas Fagan, dedicated May 20, 2010, in a central fountain. The Charlotte Ledger: 12-ft statue at East 4th Street and Kings Drive.
- Wikipedia "Midtown Park (Charlotte, North Carolina)" (citing Mecklenburg County Park and Recreation + Charlotte Observer Jan 29 2012) and charlottemidtown.com: one-acre minipark at South Kings Drive and Pearl Park Way, opened spring 2012; Braille Music Box, seven-foot sphere by Po Shu Wang and Louise Bertelsen that translates Braille letters into musical notes; formal stairway to the greenway.
- Tip slide: general guidance (Mecklenburg County property records, City of Charlotte zoning map, FEMA flood map); no claim about any specific home.
