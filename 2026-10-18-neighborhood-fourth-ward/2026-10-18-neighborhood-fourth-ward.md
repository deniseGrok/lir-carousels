## Slides
1. VICTORIAN HOMES IN UPTOWN. (hook band: CHARLOTTE NC. FOURTH WARD.) The historic northwest quarter of Uptown Charlotte; about 30 city blocks, anchored by Fourth Ward Park; bordered by Tryon St., Trade St. and historic Elmwood Cemetery (A)
2. A NEIGHBORHOOD BROUGHT BACK. One of four wards Charlotte created in the mid-1830s; a Junior League restoration program in 1976 sparked its revival; it began with the Berryhill House (1884, Victorian Italianate) (A)
3. QUEEN ANNE TO CRAFTSMAN. Queen Anne, Victorian Italianate and Folk Victorian homes; Craftsman bungalows and Colonial Revival too; several homes were moved here and restored in the 1970s; some keep their original heart pine floors and fireplaces (A)
4. TURRETS, SLATE AND PORCHES. (tag QUEEN ANNE STYLE) Overcarsh House (1879): round corner turret, sunburst gables; Liddell-McNinch House: fish-scale and diamond slate roof; Sheppard House (1898): slate roof and stained glass (A)
5. FOUNTAINS UNDER THE TREES. (tag FOURTH WARD PARK) About three acres at 301 N. Poplar Street; fountains, walking paths and a playground; ceramic birdhouses by artist Joan Bankemper (A, C, D)
6. CHARLOTTE'S EARLIEST CHAPTER. (tag SETTLERS' CEMETERY) Charlotte's first city burial ground, in use by 1776; Revolutionary War leaders Thomas Polk and George Graham rest here; a quiet urban park with walkways, benches and a fountain (B)
7. DINNER WITH A HISTORY. Alexander Michael's: a tavern since 1983, in an 1897 store; McNinch House Restaurant: multi-course dinners in a Victorian home since 1989; both serve Tuesday through Saturday (A, E, F)
8. STEEPLES AND STONE. (tag LANDMARKS) First Presbyterian Church: Gothic Revival, on West Trade Street; St. Peter's Episcopal Church: 1890s brick and brownstone; McColl Center for Art + Innovation (1926, Gothic Revival) (A, G)
9. WHAT HOMES COST HERE (Realtor.com, as of October 2026): median list $345,000; median sold $371,000; $408/sq ft; 100 active listings; median 76 days on market (H)

## LinkedIn
Fourth Ward is one of the four wards Charlotte was divided into in the mid-1830s, and today it is a historic district of about 30 city blocks in Uptown. After decades of decline, a 1976 Junior League restoration program brought it back, starting with the 1884 Berryhill House. Its streets now mix restored Queen Anne and Victorian Italianate homes with condos, anchored by the three-acre Fourth Ward Park and Settlers' Cemetery, Charlotte's first municipal burial ground. Realtor.com shows a $345,000 median list price and a $371,000 median sold price as of October 2026, with 100 active listings and a median of 76 days on market. Inventory that varies this much, from historic homes to condos, is a good reason to compare property types side by side before you make an offer.
